## Climate monitoring from satellite data

1. NASA Science: Earth Science Data  
   https://science.nasa.gov/earth/data/
2. NASA Science: Landsat Data Overview  
   https://science.nasa.gov/mission/landsat/data-overview/
3. NOAA NCEI: Satellite  
   https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/satellite
4. Copernicus Climate Change Service: About us  
   https://climate.copernicus.eu/about-us
5. ESA: Climate Change Initiative  
   https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Space_for_our_climate/Climate_Change_Initiative
6. ESA Climate Office: Essential Climate Variables  
   https://climate.esa.int/en/about-us-new/climate-change-initiative/essential-climate-variables/
7. National Centre for Earth Observation: Climate Analysis  
   https://www.nceo.ac.uk/our-research/climate-analysis/
8. ESSIC, University of Maryland: Research  
   https://essic.umd.edu/research/

Usage in pages:

- Supported the framing of climate monitoring as a long-term, calibration-sensitive branch of Earth observation rather than an event-response workflow.
- Supported references to open Earth observation data access, climate data records, essential climate variables, and the role of long-term well-calibrated datasets.
- Supported the examples of how the field is practiced through broader data services and university/institute research programs.

## Scope notes

- Content was kept introductory and workflow-oriented; it avoids volatile numeric claims, annual rankings, or specific trend magnitudes.
- The page emphasizes stable infrastructure, data concepts and representative programs that should remain useful across future updates.
- A future run could deepen this branch with focused subpages on the water cycle, cryosphere, carbon monitoring or ocean climate if navigation later needs them.
