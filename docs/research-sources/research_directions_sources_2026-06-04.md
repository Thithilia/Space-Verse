Usage in pages:

- Supported the distinction between global mean sea level, regional variability, and relative sea level experienced at a local coast.
- Supported the explanation that sea-level research usually combines satellite altimetry, tide gauges, land-motion context, shoreline mapping, and hazard interpretation rather than relying on a single dataset.
- Supported references to the TOPEX/Jason/Sentinel-6 continuity record, SWOT as a higher-resolution coastal/ocean mission, and NOAA/USGS portals as operationally relevant coastal context.
- Supported the research-practice examples by showing both a mission-to-practitioner program (NASA Sea Level Change Team) and university coastal-process labs, without presenting any one lab as the full field.

## Scope notes

- Content was kept introductory and avoided quoting current projection numbers, local inundation statistics, or policy scenarios that would require location-specific treatment and tighter attribution.
- The page emphasizes the difference between global and local sea-level problems because that distinction is central to the field and easy for beginners to miss.
- A later run could split this branch further into coastal flooding, shoreline change, estuaries and deltas, saltwater intrusion, or sea-level budget methods if the top-level navigation is later reconciled.
