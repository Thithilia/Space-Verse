Usage in pages:

- Supported the framing that mountain permafrost is patchy, strongly controlled by topography and surface conditions, and should not be treated as a simple extension of broad polar lowland permafrost.
- Supported the explanation that rock glaciers are one of the clearest geomorphic expressions of mountain permafrost and that their motion can be monitored with remote sensing, especially InSAR and long time series.
- Supported the emphasis on boreholes, geophysics, ground temperatures, active layer, and monitoring networks as necessary anchors for satellite interpretation.
- Supported the hazard-oriented framing around rockfalls, debris flows, slope instability, and mountain infrastructure, while keeping claims qualitative and introductory.

## Scope notes

- Content was kept introductory and avoided quantitative claims about current Alpine failure frequencies, global mountain-permafrost area, or acceleration rates that would need tighter regional and temporal qualification.
- The page treats PERMOS and SLF as examples of practice in mountain settings, not as definitions for the whole field.
- A later run could split this branch further into rock glacier inventories and kinematics, permafrost-related slope hazards, Andean water and rock glaciers, Himalayan mountain permafrost, or mountain infrastructure on thawing ground.
