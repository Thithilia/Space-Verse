10. RIT Digital Imaging and Remote Sensing Laboratory  
    https://www.rit.edu/dirs/

Usage in page:

- Supported the framing of this page as an Earth-system-oriented branch of remote sensing rather than a duplicate of the broader remote-sensing overview.
- Supported references to long-term land observation, Copernicus Sentinel capabilities, NASA open Earth-science data access, and NOAA operational environmental products.
- Supported the examples of how university labs practice the field in different ways: one more ecosystem and land-use oriented, one more sensor and end-to-end imaging oriented.

## Scope notes

- Content was kept introductory and workflow-oriented rather than trying to summarize all of Earth-system science.
- The page avoids volatile statistics and instead emphasizes stable infrastructure, methods and representative data systems.
- A future run can deepen this branch with dedicated subpages for climate remote sensing, hydrology or coastal/ocean observation if those are later needed in navigation.

Usage in page:

- Supported the framing of disaster monitoring as a decision-support workflow across preparedness, response and recovery rather than only a list of hazards.
- Supported references to Copernicus EMS, NOAA operational satellites, USGS hazard data distribution, and JPL radar-based hazard products.
- Supported the emphasis on SAR/InSAR for all-weather flood and deformation monitoring without overgeneralizing one lab's methods to the entire field.
