## Cryosphere monitoring from satellite data

1. National Snow and Ice Data Center (NSIDC): What is the Cryosphere?  
   https://nsidc.org/learn/what-cryosphere
2. NASA Earth Observatory: Sea Ice  
   https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sea-ice
3. NASA Science: ICESat-2  
   https://science.nasa.gov/mission/icesat-2/
4. European Space Agency (ESA): CryoSat  
   https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/CryoSat
5. NASA Snow: About the Mission  
   https://snow.nasa.gov/about-mission
6. NOAA NESDIS: Sea Ice  
   https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/our-environment/ice-snow/sea-ice
7. University of Washington: Polar Science Center  
   https://research.washington.edu/centers/polar-science-center
8. Penn State Ice and Climate Research Center  
   https://www.psice.psu.edu/

Usage in pages:

- Supported the overview of cryosphere as the snow-and-ice branch of the Earth system, including sea ice, glaciers, ice sheets, snow, and frozen ground.
- Supported references to mixed observing strategies such as passive microwave, altimetry, lidar, optical imagery, field campaigns, and model integration.
- Supported representative mission examples including ICESat-2, CryoSat, and SnowEx, plus operational sea-ice monitoring from NOAA.
- Supported the research-group examples as illustrations of how cryosphere work spans observation, field measurement, modeling, and sea-level or polar-process studies.
