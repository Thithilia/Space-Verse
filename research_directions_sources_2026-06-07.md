Usage in pages:

- Supported the distinction between global methane mapping, hotspot detection, and facility-scale plume attribution so the new page would not overstate what one sensor can do alone.
- Supported references to TROPOMI as a wide-area mapper, EMIT as a public methane-plume dataset, and Tanager-1 / Carbon Mapper as a facility-scale open-data workflow.
- Supported the explanation that practical methane mitigation workflows often combine multiple satellites, tip-and-cue strategies, and explicit alert/response systems rather than standalone imagery.
- Supported the research-practice examples by combining a broad agency mission view, an intergovernmental operational platform, and official research-group or monitoring-organization pages.

## Scope notes

- Content was kept introductory and avoided quoting current global plume counts, country rankings, or sector-by-sector emission totals that would require tighter temporal attribution and ongoing verification.
- MethaneSAT was not used in the page because its mission status changed after loss of contact announced in June 2025; the page instead relies on still-active or still-publicly updated official sources.
- A later run could split this branch further into landfill methane, oil-and-gas basin monitoring, coal mine methane, wetland methane inversions, or methane alert operations once the top-level navigation is reconciled.
