   https://www.usgs.gov/publications/thermokarst-lakes-drainage-and-drained-basins-0
2. NASA Earth Observatory: Ice and Snow

   https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/nasa-flights-detect-millions-of-arctic-methane-hotspots/

Usage in pages:

- Supported the explanation that thermokarst lakes belong to a broader cycle of lake formation, expansion, drainage, and drained-basin reorganization in ice-rich permafrost lowlands.
- Supported the distinction between landscape mapping from satellites and stronger carbon or methane claims that require field campaigns, airborne data, geophysics, cores, or gas measurements.
- Supported references to ABoVE as a stable programmatic frame linking permafrost, hydrology, thermokarst activity, land deformation, methane bubble emissions, and landscape vulnerability.
- Supported the research-practice examples by combining broad official agency sources with one university project page describing how thermokarst work is actually assembled across methods.

## Scope notes

- Content was kept introductory and avoided current counts of lakes, hotspot totals, annual methane fluxes, or claims about Arctic-wide trend direction that would require tighter geographic and temporal qualification.
- The page avoids turning one UAF project into a field-wide definition; the UAF page is used as one example of practice, while USGS and NASA sources anchor the broader framing.
- A later run could split this branch further into drained lake basins, winter methane bubble detection, yedoma thermokarst, tundra-fire thermokarst interactions, or Arctic coastal thermokarst if the parent navigation is later reconciled.
